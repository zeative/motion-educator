f1Wound healing is usually described as four overlapping phases: hemostasis (bleeding stops, minutes), inflammation (cleaning, from the first hours to about day 3: the first 72 h), proliferation (new tissue and new skin, about day 3 to week 3) and remodelling (from about week 3 to a year or more; sources range up to two years). The film paints them on the time bar as 1 to 10 minutes, 10 minutes to 3 days, 3 to 21 days and 21 days to a year. Simplification: the phases overlap, and the times are for a small, clean wound in healthy skin.StatPearls, Wound Healing Phases (Wallace, Basehore, Zito; NCBI Bookshelf NBK470443, PubMed 29262065); StatPearls, Physiology, Wound Healing (NBK535406). Read as search summaries: the pages themselves could not be opened from this session.
f2Platelets stick to the exposed wall of a torn vessel and to each other, forming a platelet plug; thrombin turns the blood protein fibrinogen into fibrin, whose strands form a net that traps red blood cells and more platelets and reinforces the plug into a clot. Sizes for the drawing: a red cell is about 7 to 8 µm across, a platelet about 2 to 3 µm (drawn 44 px and 16 px), not otherwise to scale.OpenStax, Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.5 Hemostasis (openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology/pages/18-5-hemostasis), read as a search summary; cell sizes from general textbook knowledge, not looked up.
f3Within minutes: the bleeding time of a small standard skin cut is a few minutes (published normal ranges vary, roughly 1 to 9 minutes). The film says 'dalam beberapa menit' and shows no exact figure.Bleeding time reference ranges (e.g. Healthline, Bleeding Time Test; clinical laboratory lecture notes), read as search summaries.
f4White blood cells called neutrophils arrive first: the bulk are recruited in the first 6 to 12 hours, peak at about a day and stay 2 to 5 days; they engulf (phagocytose) bacteria and kill them. Macrophages, from monocytes in the blood, follow (in numbers around day 2 to 3), clear debris and spent neutrophils and signal the switch to repair. The film shows one neutrophil, labelled 'sel darah putih', at about 6 hours; macrophages are left out for time. The germs are drawn as rods, not as a particular species.Review, Immunology of Wound Healing, Current Dermatology Reports (doi 10.1007/s13671-018-0234-9); StatPearls, Physiology, Wound Healing (NBK535406). Read as search summaries.
f5In the proliferation phase (about day 3 to week 3), fibroblasts lay down new matrix and new capillaries grow in, forming granulation tissue that fills the wound from its base.StatPearls, Wound Healing Phases (NBK470443); read as a search summary.
f6Skin cells (keratinocytes) at the wound edge change within about 6 hours of injury and start migrating across the wound by about 18 hours in human skin (re-epithelialization), moving over the new tissue and under the scab until the edges meet. Simplification: the film shows the crawl after the new tissue for clarity; in reality the two overlap.Paladini et al., Onset of re-epithelialization after skin injury correlates with a reorganization of keratin filaments in wound edge keratinocytes, Journal of Cell Biology 132 (3): 381, 1996 (read as a search summary).
f7In remodelling, the first collagen (type III) is replaced by type I and the fibres are reorganised; this goes on for months, a year or more. The repaired skin is weaker: about 20% of its strength at three weeks, 70 to 80% by three months, and at most about 75 to 80% of unwounded skin. The film writes '≤ 80%'.Review on scar biology in PMC (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5792236), read as a search summary; Gurtner et al., Wound repair and regeneration, Nature 453: 314, 2008 (from knowledge, not looked up).
f8The time bar is a log scale from 1 minute to 1 year (525 600 minutes) over 860 px: x = 64 + 860 x log10(minutes) / log10(525 600). An hour sits at x 331, a day at 539, a week at 666, a year at 924; 10 minutes at 214, 3 days at 611, 21 days at 738; the marker's 6 hours at 448. Its chips: 'beberapa menit' (f3), '≈ 6 jam' (f4), 'hari ke-3 sampai ke-21' (f1, f5) and 'berbulan-bulan' (f7). The marker's positions in between are illustrative within each phase's window.Arithmetic from f1, f3, f4, f5, f7.
f9Scar tissue differs from the skin around it: its collagen lies in parallel bundles instead of a basket weave, and the junction with the epidermis is flat (no wavy ridges); it has no hair follicles or sweat glands. The film draws parallel fibres, a flat base and a paler band. The narration says 'kadang tersisa' (sometimes remains) because a small, shallow scrape often leaves no visible scar.Gurtner et al., Nature 453: 314, 2008; general dermatology textbooks.
