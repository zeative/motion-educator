Gimana lebah bikin madu?Madu sekental ini, awalnya cuma cairan bunga yang encer banget. Kok bisa berubah?

PromiseMadu itu nektar bunga yang diolah lebah: enzim dari tubuh lebah memotong gulanya jadi dua gula yang lebih kecil, lalu lebah mengipasi nektar di sel lilin sampai airnya tinggal sekitar seperlima, baru selnya ditutup lilin. Karena airnya sedikit, madu kental dan awet.
AudienceOrang Indonesia yang nonton video pendek dan biasa minum madu. Mereka tahu madu dibuat lebah dari bunga, tapi banyak yang mengira bunga 'punya madu' dan lebah tinggal mengangkutnya, atau nggak tahu kenapa madu bisa kental dan awet.

f1Nectar is mostly water with sugar dissolved in it. The sugar varies greatly between plants, usually 25-50 % (so 50-75 % water); 'water usually makes up approximately 70 percent of nectar'; nectar 'can contain up to 80 percent water'. The film's worked example is kaliandra nectar at 20 % sugar (80 % water, f2): the s03 column is 512 px of water over 128 px of sugar (80 : 20), and the recap says 'air ±80 %'.F. Ratnieks, 'How Bees Make Honey', information sheet, Laboratory of Apiculture and Social Insects, University of Sussex (2011); M. Shipman, 'How Do Bees Make Honey?', NC State University News (19 June 2013); S. Collins-Smith, 'How is honey made?', Mississippi State University Extension.
f2Kaliandra (Calliandra calothyrsus) is grown in Java as a nectar source for honey (Perum Perhutani planted it in state forest for honey production; 1,625 ha in the Sukabumi district in 1989). Its nectar has been measured at 20-50 µl a flower with 14-20 % sugar. Bees visit for the nectar left after the night's flowering, clinging to the side of the floral cup and foraging at the base of the stamens and corolla, as s04 draws. Monofloral kaliandra honey from Apis mellifera has been sampled in Sukabumi, West Java. Simplification: the film shows an Apis mellifera worker; in the older Java reports the kaliandra honey came from Apis cerana hives.D. J. Chamberlain (ed.), 'Calliandra calothyrsus: an agroforestry tree for the humid tropics', Tropical Forestry Paper 40, Oxford Forestry Institute (2001), ch. 3 (nectar volume and sugar) and 5.7 'Honey production' (bees as nectar robbers on the floral cup; Perhutani; Apis cerana); R. A. Rosyidah, A. E. Z. Hasan and D. Andrianto, 'Antibacterial and diastase enzyme activities of honey Apis mellifera from Indonesia', BIOTROPIA 31(1): 23-33 (2024), sample list (Calliandra calothyrsus, Sukabumi, West Java).
f3A forager drinks nectar and stores it in its crop, the honey stomach, a storage sac in the abdomen that visibly lengthens the abdomen when full (drawn at its front, before the valve, as anatomy diagrams show it); the crop is used for storage and the nectar is not digested there. The proventriculus, a valve at the back of the honey stomach, controls what passes on to the midgut. On screen: 'kantong madu', 'katup', 'cuma disimpan, nggak dicerna'. Drawn in profile from the worker's anatomy: head, thorax, abdomen, two pairs of wings, six legs, elbowed antennae, the proboscis.Arizona State University, Ask A Biologist, 'Honey Bee Anatomy' (askabiologist.asu.edu/honey-bee-anatomy): honey stomach (foregut/crop) 'a storage sac, used in honey bees to carry nectar'; proventriculus 'can control the flow of nectar and solids. This allows honey bees to store nectar in the honey stomach without being digested'; NC State News (as f1): 'The crop is used solely for storage, and the bee does not digest the nectar at all'; University of Sussex LASI (as f1): 'When full ... the forager's abdomen is visibly longer'.
f4Bees add an enzyme, invertase, to the nectar (foragers as they swallow it into the honey stomach, and processor bees each time they regurgitate it). Invertase breaks each sucrose molecule (table sugar, the main sugar of most nectar) into two smaller sugars, glucose and fructose. s07 draws sucrose as a glucose hexagon joined by an oxygen to a fructose pentagon. Simplification: the film shows one enzyme; the bees also add glucose oxidase later, which the film leaves out.Mississippi State University Extension (as f1): 'Foragers collecting nectar from flowers add an enzyme (invertase) as they swallow it ... breaking down the nectar's complex sugar (sucrose) into simpler sugars (glucose and fructose) within the honey stomach'; University of Sussex LASI (as f1): 'The bees make an enzyme that breaks each sucrose molecule into two smaller sugar molecules, one glucose and one fructose'; OpenStax Biology 2e, 3.2 'Carbohydrates' (sucrose is a glucose and a fructose joined by a glycosidic bond).
f5Back at the hive a forager regurgitates its nectar load to a receiver (processor, food-handling) worker, which takes it with her tongue extended; the forager goes back to the flowers.University of Sussex LASI (as f1), figure b: 'On returning to the hive a nectar forager (right) transfers her nectar to a receiver worker (left) who has her tongue extended'; NC State News and Mississippi State University Extension (as f1).
f6The receiver bee puts the nectar into the hexagonal beeswax cells of the comb (it takes many loads to fill a cell).NC State News (as f1): 'regurgitates it into a hexagonal wax cell ... it takes many loads of nectar to fill a cell'; Mississippi State University Extension and University of Sussex LASI (as f1).
f7To ripen the nectar the bees dry it out: they fan their wings, which moves air over the comb and helps the water evaporate. Simplification: bees also work droplets with their mouthparts to evaporate water, which the film does not show.Mississippi State University Extension (as f1): 'The bees also reduce the water content of the nectar by rapidly beating or fanning their wings in the hive'; NC State News (as f1): 'fanning their wings, which creates airflow around the honeycomb and helps water evaporate'; University of Sussex LASI (as f1).
f8Ripe honey holds about a fifth of its weight in water: about 17 % (LASI) or 18 % (MSU); the Codex standard allows at most 20 % for most honeys; Indonesian Apis mellifera honeys measured 17.5-22 % (the Indonesian standard, SNI, allows up to 22 %). The voice says 'sekitar seperlima'; the screen says 'air ±20 %'. Worked example: 100 g of nectar at 20 % sugar holds 20 g of sugar and 80 g of water; at 20 % water the honey weighs 20 / 0.8 = 25 g (20 g sugar + 5 g water), so 75 g of water evaporated. The s12 columns at 7 px a gram: 700 px (water 560, sugar 140) and 175 px (water 35, sugar 140).University of Sussex LASI (as f1): 'approximately 83% sugar and 17% water'; Mississippi State University Extension: 'around 18 percent'; Codex Alimentarius, Standard for Honey CXS 12-1981 (Rev. 2001), 3.4 Moisture content: 'Honeys not listed below - not more than 20%'; Rosyidah et al., BIOTROPIA 31(1) 2024 (as f2): water content 17.50-22.00 %, kaliandra honey 22.00 %, 'determined by SNI'. The arithmetic is in storyboard.build.py.
f9When the nectar has ripened into honey, the bees cap the cell with a wax seal.NC State News (as f1): 'the processor bees "cap" the cell with an airtight wax seal'; University of Sussex LASI (as f1): 'The cell is then capped with wax'.
f10Ripe honey contains so little water and so much sugar that yeasts and other microbes cannot grow in it, so it keeps; but honey takes up moisture from the air, so it must stay sealed (capped in the comb, the lid on the jar), and honey with too much water can ferment. On screen: 'awet, asal tertutup rapat' and 'air sedikit: mikroba susah tumbuh'.NC State News (as f1): 'it contains so little water that no microbes can grow in it ... If the cap is not airtight, the honey will absorb moisture ... why it's important to screw the lid back on your jar of honey'; University of Sussex LASI (as f1): 'too concentrated for yeasts and other microorganisms to grow'; Rosyidah et al. 2024 (as f2) on fermentation of honey with high water content.
