Kenapa popcorn bisa meletus?Kenapa biji jagung yang keras dan kelihatan kering bisa meletus jadi popcorn?

PromiseDi dalam biji popcorn ada sedikit air (sekitar 14% beratnya). Waktu dipanasin, airnya jadi uap yang ketahan kulit yang keras dan rapat, sampai di sekitar 180 °C tekanannya kira-kira 9 kali tekanan udara; kulitnya jebol, uapnya nyembur (itulah bunyi 'pop'-nya), dan pati yang lembek mengembang jadi busa yang mengeras.
AudienceOrang Indonesia yang nonton video pendek, dari anak sekolah sampai orang dewasa yang suka ngemil popcorn di bioskop atau bikin sendiri pakai wajan. Mereka tahu popcorn meletus kalau dipanasin, tapi nggak tahu apa yang bikin bijinya meledak.

IdClaimSource
p1A popcorn kernel has three parts: the hull (pericarp), the starchy endosperm and the germ. It holds a small amount of water in its starch: the ideal moisture is about 13.5 to 14 % of the kernel's weight. The film says 'sekitar empat belas persen beratnya' and writes 'AIR ±14%'. Simplification: the water is spread through the starch as moisture; the film draws it as a few blue drops.The Popcorn Board, 'What Makes Popcorn Pop?' (popcorn.org/All-About-Popcorn/What-Makes-Popcorn-Pop): germ, endosperm, pericarp; 'a small drop of water stored inside a circle of soft starch'; 13.5-14 % moisture. Iowa State University, The Maize Page (popcorn FAQ, mirrored by the University of Hamburg): 'The optimum moisture is 14%'. Purdue University College of Agriculture, 'What's under the shell of this popular snack?' (2020, B. Hamaker): moisture about 13 to 14.5 %.
p2The kernel looks dry and is hard, yet it contains that water (p1): the surprise the film opens on.As p1; USDA National Agricultural Library, exhibit 'Popcorn: How does popcorn pop?' (nal.usda.gov/exhibits/speccoll/exhibits/show/popcorn): each kernel holds water in soft starch inside a hard outer surface.
p3When the kernel is heated, its water turns to steam; the hard hull holds it in and acts as a pressure vessel, so the pressure inside rises until the hull gives way. The hull of good popping kernels is a strong, highly ordered cellulose layer that keeps the moisture in; press coverage of that work likens it to a pressure cooker (the film's 'kayak panci presto').Hoseney, Zeleznak & Abdelrahman, 'Mechanism of popcorn popping', Journal of Cereal Science 1 (1983) 43-52, doi 10.1016/S0733-5210(83)80007-1 (the pericarp acts as a pressure vessel); Tandjung, Janaswamy, Chandrasekaran & Hamaker, Biomacromolecules (2005), reported by the American Chemical Society release of 15 April 2005 (EurekAlert, ScienceDaily: 'the corn pericarp acts like a pressure cooker'); USDA NAL exhibit (as p2).
p4Nearly all kernels pop at a critical temperature of about 180 °C, whatever their size. Hoseney et al. measured popping at about 177 °C. The film says 'sekitar seratus delapan puluh derajat' and the thermometer (0 to 200 °C) stands at 180, 90% of its scale.Virot & Ponomarenko, 'Popcorn: critical temperature, jump and sound', Journal of the Royal Society Interface 12 (2015) 20141247, doi 10.1098/rsif.2014.1247 (abstract; PMC4345489); Hoseney et al. 1983 (as p3).
p5At popping the pressure inside the kernel is about 135 psi, that is 931 kPa (the vapour pressure of water at about 177 °C). One standard atmosphere is 101.325 kPa, so 931 / 101.325 = 9.19: the film says 'kira-kira sembilan kali tekanan udara' and writes '≈ 9× TEKANAN UDARA' and '931 kPa · 135 psi'; the gauge's needle stands at 9.2 of 10. In s12 the cracked kernel's needle is drawn low (2 of 10): an illustration, not a measurement.Hoseney et al. 1983 (as p3), as quoted by Wu & Schwartzberg, 'Popping behavior and zein coating of popcorn', Cereal Chemistry 69 (1992) 567-573 (cerealsgrains.org): 'popping occurred at about 177 °C, which for pure water is equivalent to a pressure of 931 kPa (135 psia) inside the kernel'. One standard atmosphere = 101.325 kPa (NIST, SP 811, Guide for the Use of the SI).
p6When the hull bursts, the steam escapes and the pressure drop lets the starch, softened by the hot steam, expand into a foam that sets as it cools: the white flake, with bits of hull left at its base. The expansion happens in a flash (fracture to flake in under about 90 ms). Simplification: the film shows the softening, the swelling and the setting as three drawings.Hoseney et al. 1983 (as p3: the superheated water drives the expansion); USDA NAL exhibit (as p2: the starch inflates and turns the kernel inside out); Virot & Ponomarenko 2015 (as p4), reported by AFP/phys.org (Feb 2015): fracture to flake in less than 90 ms. The softening ('gelatinous') and setting of the starch are as described in EBSCO Research Starters, 'Popcorn': a secondary source, not checked against a primary paper.
p7The 'pop' you hear is not the hull cracking: by synchronising high-speed video with sound recordings, Virot & Ponomarenko attribute the sound to the release of the water vapour from the kernel.Virot & Ponomarenko 2015 (as p4), abstract: 'By synchronizing video and acoustic recordings, the familiar pop is attributed to the release of water vapour'; Royal Society blog, 'The science of popcorn' (Feb 2015).
p8Kernels whose hull is cut, cracked or weak lose their moisture as steam during heating instead of building pressure, so they do not pop (the unpopped kernels at the bottom of the bag).American Chemical Society release on Tandjung et al. 2005 (as p3) and Purdue University College of Agriculture (as p1): kernels with cuts or weak spots in the pericarp let the moisture leave on heating and fail to pop.
