Kenapa ada musim?Kenapa pas Desember Eropa bersalju tapi Australia musim panas, dan kenapa Indonesia nggak punya musim dingin?

PromiseMusim terjadi karena sumbu Bumi miring sekitar 23,4°, bukan karena jaraknya ke Matahari: belahan yang condong ke Matahari dapat sinar lebih tegak dan siang lebih panjang. Indonesia ada di dekat khatulistiwa, jadi Matahari siangnya tetap tinggi sepanjang tahun.
AudienceAnak sekolah dan orang dewasa di Indonesia yang nonton video pendek. Mereka tahu negara lain punya empat musim, dan banyak yang mengira itu karena Bumi kadang lebih dekat, kadang lebih jauh dari Matahari.

f1Earth's axis is tilted 23.44 degrees to its orbit (its obliquity). The film says 'sekitar dua puluh tiga koma empat derajat' and writes '23,4°'.NASA, Earth Fact Sheet (nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html): 'Obliquity to orbit (deg) 23.44'.
f2Earth is closest to the Sun (perihelion, about 147.1 million km) in early January and farthest (aphelion, about 152.1 million km) in early July. The difference is small, and the Northern Hemisphere's winter comes when Earth is closest, so distance does not cause the seasons. The film's oval in s03 is exaggerated and labelled 'bukan skala'.NASA, Earth Fact Sheet (perihelion 147.095, aphelion 152.100 million km); U.S. Naval Observatory, 'The Seasons and the Earth's Orbit' (aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/seasons_orbit): perihelion in early January; NASA Space Place, 'What Causes the Seasons?'.
f3The seasons come from the tilt: the axis keeps pointing the same way as Earth orbits, so in June the Northern Hemisphere leans towards the Sun and in December the Southern Hemisphere does.NASA Space Place, 'What Causes the Seasons?' (spaceplace.nasa.gov/seasons); NOAA NESDIS, 'Why does Earth have seasons?'; USNO (as f2).
f4The hemispheres have opposite seasons: when it is winter in Europe (December), it is summer in Australia.NOAA NESDIS, 'Why does Earth have seasons?' (nesdis.noaa.gov); NASA Space Place (as f3).
f5The hemisphere leaning towards the Sun gets more direct sunlight, which concentrates the same light on a smaller area, and longer days; slanting light spreads over a bigger area and warms it less. The torch in s09 and s11 shows the spreading. The clock in s09b shows the day at about 50 degrees north in late June: about 16 of 24 hours of daylight (Berlin, 52.5 degrees north: about 16 h 50 min on 21 June).NASA Space Place, 'What Causes the Seasons?'; NOAA NESDIS, 'Why does Earth have seasons?'. f6Indonesia lies astride the equator, between about 6° north and 11° south. Jakarta is at about 6.2° south.Indonesia's astronomical position, 6° N to 11° S (the Indonesian government's description, reported by theindonesia.id, 2021); Britannica, 'Malay Archipelago' (its islands lie within about 10 degrees of the Equator).
f7The Sun's height at noon is 90° minus the difference between the latitude and the Sun's declination, which swings between +23.44° and −23.44° through the year. Jakarta (6.2° S): its lowest noon Sun is 90 − (23.44 + 6.2) ≈ 60°. Berlin (52.5° N) in late December: 90 − (52.52 + 23.44) ≈ 14°.
