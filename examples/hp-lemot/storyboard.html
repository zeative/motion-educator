IdClaimSource
f1Phone storage is NAND flash. Its smallest unit for reading and writing (programming) is the page; its smallest unit for erasing is the block, which holds many pages. Typical figures: pages of about 4 to 16 KB, blocks of 64 to 512 pages or more (one datasheet: 16 384-byte pages, 256 pages a block, about 4 MB). The film draws 16 pages a block and says on screen that a real block has hundreds.Phison / NYCU course slides on NAND flash ('Page is the smallest unit for read and program; Block is the smallest unit for erase', 1 block = 256 pages of 16 KB); flashdba.com, 'Understanding Flash: Blocks, Pages and Program / Erases' (2014); a 64 Gb MLC NAND datasheet (16 384-byte page, 256 pages a block). Read through search summaries; the pages themselves could not be opened from this session.
f2A NAND page that holds data cannot be overwritten in place: it must be erased first, and erasing works only on a whole block. So changed data is written to an already-erased page and the old copy is marked stale (no longer used).Phison / NYCU slides ('Must erase before program (cannot overwrite)'); Hu et al., 'Write Amplification Analysis in Flash-Based Solid State Drives', SYSTOR 2009 (IBM Zurich); flashdba.com (2014). Read through search summaries.
f3When free space runs out, there is no erased page to write to until the controller makes one by garbage collection. Simplification: the film shows the six blocks with no empty page at all; a real controller keeps hidden spare blocks (over-provisioning) and collects garbage in the background, but when the drive is nearly full it has to do it more often and while you wait.Hu et al., SYSTOR 2009; Stanford CS111 lecture notes on flash memory (garbage collection and TRIM). Read through search summaries.
f4Garbage collection: the controller picks a block (usually the one with the fewest valid pages), copies its still-valid pages to an erased block, then erases the old block. Those copies are writes nobody asked for (write amplification). In the film one photo is drawn as one page; a real photo of a few megabytes fills hundreds of pages. The 11 pages moved in s07 match the toy model's average at 97% full (10,7 pages a clean-up).Hu et al., SYSTOR 2009 (greedy reclaiming); IBM Research report RZ3757; sim.py in this folder for the 10,7.
f5Write amplification grows as spare space shrinks: with less room, the blocks being cleaned hold more valid pages, so more must be copied, and clean-ups are needed more often. In the toy flash of sim.py (7 blocks of 16 pages, greedy garbage collection, random overwrites) the pages written per page of new data are 1,29 at 60% full, 1,82 at 80%, 2,32 at 90% and 3,00 at 97%, and clean-ups per write rise from 0,08 to 0,19. The film shows these as 1,3×, 1,8×, 2,3× and 3×, labelled as a simple model, not a measured phone.Hu et al., SYSTOR 2009 ('The impact of garbage collection on write amplification is influenced by the level of over-provisioning'); 'Modelling and Managing SSD Write-amplification' (arXiv 1504.00229); the arithmetic: sim.py in this folder (200 000 writes a level, seed 1).
f6The system and apps keep temporary (cache) files on the same storage, and Android may delete cache files when the device is low on internal storage. So a nearly full phone also has less room for the temporary files apps and the system use while working.Android developer documentation on app-specific files ('When the device is low on internal storage space, Android may delete these cache files to recover space'), quoted via a forum post because developer.android.com could not be opened from this session.
f7Google's Android help says a phone can start having issues when less than 10% of its storage is free. So the film says 'paling nggak sepuluh persen' (at least about ten percent) and shows '≥ 10% kosong'. Higher figures (15 to 20%) appear only in secondary sources and are not used.Google, Android Help, 'Speed up a slow Android device' (support.google.com/android/answer/7667018), as quoted in search results; the page itself could not be opened from this session.
f8TRIM: the phone's system tells the flash which pages belong to deleted files, so garbage collection need not copy them; Android has run this (fstrim) automatically during idle maintenance since Android 4.3 (2013). It cannot help when the storage is truly full of data you keep. Not spoken in the film; it is why deleting files really frees space for the controller. The percentages on the phone's screen (91%, 97%, 80%) are an illustration of a phone filling up.AnandTech, 'Android 4.3 Update Brings TRIM to All Nexus Devices' (2013); Wikipedia, Trim (computing). Read through search summaries.
