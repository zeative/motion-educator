Where does a phone battery keep its energy?A full phone battery and an empty one hold the same stuff, so where is the energy?

PromiseA phone battery isn't filled with electricity: charging pushes lithium into the graphite, where it is loosely held (like a ball pushed up a hill), and in use the lithium goes back to the metal oxide while its electrons are forced the long way round, through the phone; that flow is the current.
AudienceCurious teenagers and adults anywhere who charge a phone every day, often watching with the sound off at first. Many picture a battery as a tank that gets filled with electricity; they know the words 'ion' and 'electron' from school but have never seen what moves inside a battery.

Fact sheet

IdClaimSource
f1Phones run on lithium-ion batteries; the usual phone chemistry is lithium cobalt oxide (LiCoO₂) at the positive side and graphite at the negative side. The film calls the positive side 'metal oxide' and writes '(in phones: lithium cobalt oxide)' small.Battery University (Cadex Electronics), BU-205 'Types of Lithium-ion' (batteryuniversity.com/article/bu-205-types-of-lithium-ion): 'Its high specific energy makes Li-cobalt the popular choice for mobile phones, laptops and digital cameras. The battery consists of a cobalt oxide cathode and a graphite carbon anode.' UL Research Institutes, 'What Are Lithium-Ion Batteries?' (ul.org/library/what-lithium-ion-battery-factsheet): li-ion powers 'our mobile phones'.
f2Inside the cell: a negative electrode of graphite (coated on copper foil), a positive electrode of metal oxide (coated on aluminium foil), a porous polymer separator between them, and an electrolyte (a lithium salt in an organic solvent). In a phone the cell is flat: the sheets are wound and flattened into a roll (or stacked) inside a sealed pouch. Simplification: the film draws one sandwich of three layers and leaves out the foils and the double-sided coating.Battery University BU-204 'How do Lithium Batteries Work?' (batteryuniversity.com/article/bu-204-how-do-lithium-batteries-work): 'a metal oxide positive electrode (cathode) that is coated onto an aluminum current collector, a negative electrode (anode) made from carbon/graphite coated on a copper current collector, a separator and electrolyte made of lithium salt in an organic solvent'; BU-301a 'Types of Battery Cells': prismatic cells 'wound and flattened into a pseudo-prismatic jelly roll ... predominantly found in mobile phones'; the pouch cell; BU-306 'What is the Function of the Separator?' (a polyolefin film, commonly about 10 to 25 µm thick).
f3Lithium ions shuttle between the two electrodes: on charge from the oxide to the graphite, on discharge back again; in the graphite they sit between its layers (intercalation: about one lithium for six carbon atoms); the electrodes are not used up as they are in most other batteries (a little lithium is lost over hundreds of cycles, f7). The film's six ions are a stand-in for billions.Battery University BU-204: 'During discharge, the ions flow from the anode to the cathode through the electrolyte and separator; charge reverses the direction' and 'It takes six carbon (graphite) atoms to bind to a single lithium ion'; The Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2019, popular information (nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2019/popular-information/): 'the lithium ions flow back and forth between the electrodes', 'intercalated in the molecular layers in graphite'.
f4Charging is driven from outside: the charger's voltage supplies electrons to the graphite through the external wire while the lithium ions move through the electrolyte into the graphite. It does not happen by itself (non-spontaneous).LG Energy Solution, Battery Inside, 'How Lithium-ion Batteries Work: An Electrochemical Explanation' (inside.lgensol.com, 2026): 'During charging ... electrons are supplied to the anode through an external power source, while lithium ions move through the electrolyte and are reinserted into the anode ... charging is a non-spontaneous electrochemical process driven by an externally applied voltage.' Nobel 2019 popular information: 'When he charged the petroleum coke with electrons, the lithium ions were drawn into the material.'
f5Where the energy is: lithium in the graphite is loosely bound; in the oxide it is bonded much more strongly, so its move back to the oxide is energetically downhill and releases energy, and only a high enough charging voltage can drive it back up. The film's hill is an analogy (gravity stands in for chemical bonding), not a measured energy diagram.S. H. Finkelstein, M. Ricci, T. Bötticher, K. Schmidt-Rohr, 'How lithium-ion batteries work conceptually: thermodynamics of Li bonding in idealized electrodes', Physical Chemistry Chemical Physics, 2024, doi:10.1039/d4cp00818a (abstract via Europe PMC): 'compared to loosely-bound lithium in the negative electrode (anode), lithium in the ionic positive electrode is more strongly bonded, moves there in an energetically downhill irreversible process ... Only a sufficiently high charging voltage can drive it back.' LG Energy Solution (as f4): 'Because discharge proceeds toward a lower-energy state, it occurs spontaneously.' Nobel 2019: the ions flow 'towards the cobalt oxide in the cathode, which has a much higher potential'.
f6The separator lets lithium ions through its pores but does not conduct electrons, and the electrolyte in its pores carries ions but not electrons, so on discharge the electrons must travel through the outside circuit (the phone) to reach the oxide; that flow of electrons is the electric current that powers the device. (The film stops its electron at a pore like the one the ion used, so the block reads as 'not a conductor', not as 'too big'.)Battery University BU-306: 'Although ions pass freely between the electrodes, the separator is an isolator with no electrical conductivity.' LG Energy Solution (as f4): 'the separator blocks the internal movement of electrons, ensuring that current flows through the external circuit'; LG Energy Solution, 'Lithium-Ion Battery's Structure and How It Works' (inside.lgensol.com, 2022): the electrolyte must 'prevent the electrons from entering the electrolyte and make them only move along the external conducting wire'. UL Research Institutes: 'Electrons move in the opposite direction in the external circuit. This migration is the reason the battery powers the device.'
f7A battery does not store electricity (or extra electrons): it turns electrical energy into chemical energy and back. A full and an empty phone battery hold the same materials and the same lithium; only where the lithium sits differs. Simplifications, kept off screen: over hundreds of cycles a little lithium is lost to side reactions; and the stored energy has a mass equivalent (E = mc²), which for an example 5 000 mAh × 3.87 V cell is 19.35 Wh = 69 660 J, / (3.0 × 10⁸ m/s)² ≈ 7.7 × 10⁻¹³ kg, less than a nanogram, far below anything a scale could detect.LG Energy Solution (as f4), Key Q&A: 'A battery does not store electricity itself. Instead, it is an electrochemical system that converts electrical energy into chemical energy for storage, then converts it back into electrical energy when needed.' Nobel 2019 popular information (ions flow back and forth without reacting with their surroundings). The mass arithmetic is ours (the cell is an example, not a claim in the film).
f8'Empty' is when the lithium has moved back to the oxide. Simplification: a phone stops at a cut-off voltage (Battery University gives a usable range of about 3.70 to 2.80 V per cell) before every last lithium has left the graphite, and some lithium is never moved; the film says 'once it's all home' and draws all six back.Battery University BU-204: 'the flat discharge curve offers effective utilization of the stored energy in a desirable and flat voltage spectrum of 3.70–2.80V/cell'; the direction of the ions on discharge as f3.
