Kenapa es malah ngapung di air?Kenapa sih es malah ngapung di air?

PromiseBenda padat biasanya tenggelam di cairannya sendiri, tapi waktu air membeku, tiap molekulnya menggandeng empat tetangga jadi kisi segi enam yang berongga. Molekul yang sama makan tempat sekitar sembilan persen lebih banyak, jadi es lebih ringan dari air (0,917 lawan 1,000 g/cm³) dan ngapung dengan kira-kira sembilan per sepuluh badannya di bawah permukaan.
AudienceRemaja dan orang dewasa di Indonesia yang menonton video pendek di ponsel. Mereka tahu es ngapung di gelas, tapi kebanyakan mengira itu biasa saja, atau karena 'es ada udaranya'. Belum tahu bahwa benda padat biasanya tenggelam di cairannya sendiri.

IdClaimSource
f1Ordinary ice (ice Ih) at 0 °C and normal pressure has a density of about 0,9167 g/cm³; liquid water at 0 °C about 0,9998 g/cm³. The film writes these as 0,917 and 1,000 g/cm³ (a cubic centimetre of ice weighs 0,917 g, of water 1,000 g).CRC Handbook of Chemistry and Physics (ice 0,9167 g/cm³ at 0 °C), as cited by Wikipedia, 'Ice'; IEEE Technology Navigator, 'Ice' (917 kg/m³ against 999,8 kg/m³); International Critical Tables via Journal of Glaciology (0,9168 ± 0,0005 g/ml).
f2Liquid water is densest at about 4 °C (3,98 °C, 0,99997 g/cm³); between 0 °C (0,9998) and 4 °C it changes by less than 0,02 %, so '1,000 g/cm³' holds for the cold water around the ice.Encyclopaedia Britannica, 'Water: physical properties' (maximum density 1,0000 g/cm³ at 3,98 °C); IAPWS / VSMOW figure 0,99997495 g/mL at 3,983 °C as tabulated in Wikipedia, 'Properties of water'.
f3In ice Ih each water molecule is hydrogen-bonded to four neighbours arranged as a tetrahedron (O–O about 2,76 Å, angles near 109,5°); the oxygens form puckered layers of six-membered rings, and seen down the c axis the lattice is a honeycomb with open hexagonal channels. Each bond carries one hydrogen (the ice rules, Bernal–Fowler 1933, Pauling 1935). This open framework is why ice is less dense than the liquid, where molecules also sit in the spaces the lattice leaves open. The film builds its lattice from the real geometry (hexagonal diamond, c/a = 1,63) and orients the hydrogens by the ice rules; balls are not to scale with the gaps (drawn smaller than the atoms really are, so the bonds can be seen). The film draws 240 molecules: a block 6 a wide, 5 √3 a tall and one cell (c) deep holds 6 x 5 x 8 = 240 of them.M. Chaplin, Water Structure and Science, London South Bank University: 'Hexagonal ice (ice Ih)' (water.lsbu.ac.uk/water/hexagonal_ice.html, ice_phases.html); IEEE Technology Navigator, 'Ice'; L. Pauling, J. Am. Chem. Soc. 57, 2680 (1935).
f4Freezing, the same mass of water takes about 9 % more room: volume ratio 0,9998 / 0,9167 = 1,0907, so +9,1 %. Put the other way, ice is 1 − 0,9167 / 0,9998 = 8,3 % less dense. The caption says '9% lebih banyak' of the room, which is the 9 %; it never says 'nine percent less dense', which would be wrong (8 %). The columns of s07 keep the same width and depth, so their heights carry the volume: 606 px against 606 / 1,0907 = 555,6 px.Arithmetic from f1.
f5A floating body sinks until it pushes aside its own weight of liquid (Archimedes), so the fraction under the surface is ρ_object / ρ_liquid: for ice in fresh water near 0 °C, 0,9167 / 0,9998 = 0,917, 'about nine tenths' (written ≈ 92 %). In sea water (about 1,025 g/cm³) it is 0,894, the familiar 'nine tenths of an iceberg'. The film shows fresh water in a glass. The 250 px cube floats with 0,083 x 250 = 20,8 px above the water; the 400 px cube with 33,2 px above.OpenStax, College Physics 2e, section 11.7 'Archimedes' Principle' (fraction submerged = ρ_obj / ρ_fl); arithmetic from f1.
f6For most substances the solid is denser than its liquid, so a lump of the solid sinks in its own melt; water is one of a few exceptions (others: silicon, gallium, germanium, bismuth). The film's example is paraffin wax: solid about 0,88 to 0,93 g/cm³, molten about 0,73 to 0,78 g/cm³ (100 to 135 °C), so solid wax sinks in molten wax. The caption says 'biasanya' (usually), not 'always'.Anton Paar Wiki, 'Paraffin wax' (density of molten wax); paraffin solid density in standard tables (0,88 to 0,93 g/cm³); M. Chaplin, Water Structure and Science, 'Anomalous properties of water' (density anomaly, other substances that expand on freezing).
f7In liquid water the molecules are close together and constantly moving; hydrogen bonds still form but break and re-form within picoseconds, so there is no lasting lattice and molecules can sit in the spaces the ice lattice keeps open. Simplification: the film draws the liquid without bonds, and its motion is slowed down enormously to be visible.M. Chaplin, Water Structure and Science, London South Bank University: 'Water structure' and 'Hydrogen bonding in water'.
