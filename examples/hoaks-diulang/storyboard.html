f1The effect was first shown by Hasher, Goldstein and Toppino in 1977: people rated plausible statements (true and false) on three sessions two weeks apart; statements that were repeated across sessions were judged more valid each time, unrepeated ones were not. It is now called the illusory truth effect.Hasher, L., Goldstein, D., & Toppino, T. (1977). Frequency and the conference of referential validity. Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior, 16, 107-112 (abstract read at hasherlab.psych.utoronto.ca; full text not opened).
f2The leading explanation is processing fluency: a repeated statement is easier to process, and that ease is taken as a cue for truth. Making a statement easier to read without repeating it (higher colour contrast) also raises judged truth, a little. A meta-analysis of 70 studies found a medium-sized repetition effect (d about 0.49). Simplification: memory-based and 'referential' accounts also exist and fluency is not the whole story; the film says the brain processes it more easily and takes that for truth, which all accounts share. The path through the grass is a picture of this, not anatomy, and says so on screen.Reber, R., & Schwarz, N. (1999). Effects of perceptual fluency on judgments of truth. Consciousness and Cognition, 8, 338-342; Dechêne, A., Stahl, C., Hansen, J., & Wänke, M. (2010). The truth about the truth: a meta-analytic review of the truth effect. Personality and Social Psychology Review, 14, 238-257; Unkelbach, C., & Rom, S. C. (2017), Cognition 160. Read as search summaries, not in full.
f3A single prior exposure to a fake news headline raised how accurate people later judged it, compared with headlines they had not seen; the effect held a week later and even when the headline carried a 'disputed by fact-checkers' warning. It did not work for statements that are plainly impossible. The film says only that the headline 'lebih terasa benar' (feels more true); it gives no size, and the meters in s09 are an illustration.Pennycook, G., Cannon, T. D., & Rand, D. G. (2018). Prior exposure increases perceived accuracy of fake news. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 147(12), 1865-1880 (abstract and summaries; the full text could not be opened from here).
f4Knowing the right answer does not protect against the effect: repeating a false statement such as 'The Atlantic Ocean is the largest ocean on Earth' raised its judged truth even among people who, tested separately, knew the answer (the Pacific). The authors call it knowledge neglect. The film shows a small nudge, not belief.Fazio, L. K., Brashier, N. M., Payne, B. K., & Marsh, E. J. (2015). Knowledge does not protect against illusory truth. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 144(5), 993-1002 (abstract, PubMed 26301795).
f5Hearing a claim often is not evidence for it. Focusing on accuracy when first meeting a claim reduces the effect: people who first rated statements for truth showed no illusory truth effect, those who rated them for interest did. The film's habit (check the source before forwarding) is advice built on this, not a measured cure.Brashier, N. M., Eliseev, E. D., & Marsh, E. J. (2020). An initial accuracy focus prevents illusory truth. Cognition, 194, 104054 (abstract); Calvillo & Smelter (2020), Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications.
f6The example claim (turn off your phone tonight because of cosmic radiation from space) is a real chain-message hoax that has circulated since at least 2017; LAPAN and NASA made no such warning. The time '00.30' is from versions of the message. The fake headline in s08 is the same hoax set as a headline; the second headline in s09 is invented for the comparison and labelled as an example.Kompas.com Cek Fakta, '[HOAKS] Imbauan Matikan Ponsel dan Laptop karena Radiasi Kosmik', 24 August 2022 (read as a search summary).
f7The meters, the reading passes and the path are illustrations, not measurements: they show the direction (feeling of truth up, evidence unchanged), not a size. Each is labelled 'ilustrasi' or 'gambaran' on screen. The 'terasa benar' meter rises by less with each repeat, which is the shape reported in later work, but its values are not data.The film's own drawing; direction from f1 to f4.
f8'Efek kebenaran ilusif' is an Indonesian rendering of 'illusory truth effect', the name used in the research literature; the English name is shown beside it.Term as used in f2, f4, f5.
